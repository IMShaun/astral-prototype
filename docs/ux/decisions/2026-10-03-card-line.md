# One card line, 120 characters or fewer

## Context

Card bodies were filled from whatever long field the item had. UI library cards ran up to 506 characters of specs. User groups, Access, Strategy, and Language cards ran to two or three sentences. Cards got word dense, and some lines did not help anyone choose.

## Evidence

An audit of the Work Desk found about 60 cards over 120 characters, plus about 120 Language terms whose note would show in full. Insights, projects, and reports in list view had the same issue.

## Options

1. One card line rule. Use a short `card` field where the source is longer, and keep the full text on the detail page.
2. Clamp card text to two lines in CSS.
3. Rewrite every source field to fit.

## Decision

Option 1. The rule lives in the ux-writing style guide under Card lines. The desk reads `card` first, warns in the console over 120, and never cuts.

## Rationale

A clamp hides words mid-sentence and the style guide says warn, do not cut. Rewriting the source would lose detail the detail page needs.

## Consequences

Easier: each card says one useful thing. Detail pages keep everything.

Harder: a new item with a long field needs a `card` line written for it.

## Rejected alternative

A two-line CSS clamp on every card body.

## Review when

A card needs two facts to be picked, or the Home card budget changes.
