# Research principles

**Evidence before research.** Review what already exists before designing a new activity.

**Do not design research until you understand the decision it needs to inform.**

```
Decision
What are we deciding?

Risk
What happens if our assumption is wrong?

Unknown
What evidence is missing?

Research question
What must we learn?

Method
What evidence will answer it?

Outcome
What will we do differently depending on the result?
```

Start by agreeing the questions that need answering. Turn assumptions into research questions. Choose methods that can give reliable answers with the least unnecessary effort. Update the plan as understanding develops.

Research objectives are connected to decisions (clarity, appeal, conversion, perceptions, expectations, differences between audience groups). They are not “Do users like it?”

Qualitative rounds of roughly 4–8 participants are typical for interviews or usability testing. Surveys, A/B tests, and benchmarking generally need much larger samples. Never pretend a small qualitative study can establish population prevalence.

Distinguish:

- **Qualitative evidence** — meaning, behaviour, why
- **Quantitative measurement** — how common, how much change, how stable a score is

AI analysis must remain traceable to original evidence. Show workings through evidence links. Humans remain responsible for deciding what the organisation does.

Knowledge gaps do **not** automatically start new research.
